About us · 拉面

Slow-cooked broth,
served with a smile.

Bambam Diner is a small open-air ramen spot along the National Highway in San Marcelino. Every bowl starts with a broth we boil for 13–15 hours until it turns rich and creamy — no shortcuts, no powders. Come as you are, slurp loudly, and save room for bingsu.

We started small — a couple of tables, one big pot, and a stubborn belief that Zambales deserved real ramen without the city price tag. The lanterns went up, the neighbors came by, and they kept coming back. These days you'll find families after school runs, tricycle drivers on break, and barkadas splitting a bingsu tower at sunset.

13–15 hr broth Open-air diner Family-friendly

The broth

Bones in before sunrise, ladled out after dark. 13–15 hours of a slow, rolling boil is what turns it creamy — that's the whole secret, and it isn't a shortcut.

The chashu

Marinated overnight and rolled by hand, so it melts the moment it hits the hot broth. Prefer a kick? The spicy ground pork on the tantanmen has a loyal following.

The bingsu

Shaved snow piled high, Korean-style — our answer to the Zambales heat. The Choco Oreo disappears fastest, but the Cheesecake Snow Halo is the sleeper hit.
